Defining emotional objectives and identifying core emotions are pivotal steps in the game design process, shaping the player's experience and influencing the overall success of the game. This document explores key considerations, examples, and best practices, emphasizing a player-centric approach and recognizing the dynamic nature of emotional design.

## Core Considerations

### **Player-Centric Approach:**
- Begin by understanding the target audience. Consider the emotions that resonate with players in the specific genre and theme of the game. For instance, a horror game may aim to evoke fear and suspense, while an adventure game may focus on excitement and curiosity.

### **Narrative Synergy:**
- Align emotional objectives with the game's narrative. Ensure that the intended emotions complement the story's tone and progression. In #The_Last_of_Us, the emotional objectives of grief and survival seamlessly integrate with the post-apocalyptic narrative, enhancing player engagement.

### **Cultural Sensitivity:**
- Acknowledge and respect cultural differences in emotional expression. Emotions can be culturally nuanced, and ensuring sensitivity to diverse backgrounds is crucial. Games like #Okami draw inspiration from Japanese folklore, embedding cultural emotions within the narrative.

### **Considerations Influenced by External Factors:**
- Anticipate how external factors, such as evolving player expectations and industry trends, might influence the interpretation and significance of chosen emotions. Stay adaptable and open to adjustments based on player feedback and emerging design paradigms.

## Examples of Effective Emotional Objectives

### #Journey (Thatgamecompany):

- Emotional Objectives: Wonder, Solitude, Connection.
- The game beautifully captures a range of emotions as players embark on a solitary journey, fostering a deep sense of connection with the virtual world and other players.

### #Uncharted 4: A Thief's End (Naughty Dog):

- Emotional Objectives: Adventure, Camaraderie, Redemption.
- The game combines thrilling adventure with emotional storytelling, leveraging core emotions to create a cinematic experience that resonates with players.
### #Firewatch (Campo Santo):

- **Emotional Objectives:** Isolation, Mystery, Connection.
- The game navigates complex emotions of isolation and mystery as players take on the role of a fire lookout in the Wyoming wilderness. The emotional journey is enhanced through a unique radio-based relationship, creating a strong sense of connection.

### #To_The_Moon (Freebird Games):

- **Emotional Objectives:** Regret, Nostalgia, Resolution.
- This narrative-driven game explores the emotional journey of fulfilling a dying man's last wish. The game's focus on regret, nostalgia, and the pursuit of resolution creates a poignant and emotionally charged experience.

### #Life_is_Strange: Before the Storm (Deck Nine):

- **Emotional Objectives:** Adolescence, Friendship, Identity.
- A prequel to the original, this game delves into the emotional complexities of adolescence, exploring themes of friendship and identity. The emotional objectives align with the narrative, offering players a relatable and emotionally resonant experience.

These examples showcase the diversity of emotional objectives, demonstrating how games can effectively evoke a wide range of emotions to engage players on a deep level.

## Best Practices for Identifying Core Emotions

### **Player Empathy:**
- Encourage empathy by creating characters or situations that players can emotionally connect with. In #Life_is_Strange, relatable characters and morally challenging decisions enhance emotional engagement.
### **Thematic Consistency:**
- Ensure that the chosen emotions align with the overall theme and genre of the game. In #The_Elder_Scrolls V: Skyrim, the emotional tones of exploration and discovery align seamlessly with the fantasy RPG theme.
### **Quantifiable Metrics:**
- Establish quantifiable metrics for successful emotional engagement. Track player sentiment, emotional intensity, and the duration of emotional peaks. Games like #Undertale implement unique metrics for emotional engagement by tracking player choices and consequences.

## Dynamic Considerations

### **Narrative Evolution:**
- Acknowledge that the emphasis on specific emotions may evolve as the game progresses. A dynamic approach to emotional design allows for adjustments based on unfolding narrative elements.
### **Player Interpretation:**
- Recognize that players may interpret and connect with emotions differently. Facilitate a design that allows for diverse player responses and interpretations.

Defining emotional objectives and identifying core emotions are intricate processes that demand a deep understanding of the desired player experience. A player-centric approach, narrative synergy, cultural sensitivity, and effective examples from renowned games like #Journey and #Uncharted 4 demonstrate the importance of carefully crafting emotional objectives. Best practices, including player empathy, thematic consistency, and quantifiable metrics, guide game designers in creating emotionally resonant experiences. Dynamic considerations emphasize the need for adaptability, allowing designers to respond to external factors and evolving player dynamics. By combining these considerations and practices, designers can set the stage for a game that not only entertains but leaves a lasting emotional impact on players.
